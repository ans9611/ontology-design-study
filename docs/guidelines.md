# Design guidelines drawn from the evidence

What the three strands support about building an enterprise ontology that stays cheap to query and stays in use. Each line names its evidence and how strong it is: *measured* means the controlled experiment in this repository (RQ3); *association* means a pattern seen across the eight cases (RQ2), which is not a cause; *literature* means a cited source rather than this study's data. Numbers are from [results/RESULTS.md](results/RESULTS.md) at 10,000 persons unless stated. Guidelines are identifiers, not a sequence.

| id | guideline | evidence | strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Write the questions the graph must answer before drawing the schema, and review the schema against them. | The experiment fixed its twenty questions before any schema was written, which is what exposed the reverse-lookup failures below. Across cases, schemas built around what applications do with entities (`action-first`: eBay and Facebook in [15], Schema.org [17]) were sustained; the one built top-down from an upper ontology first (Cyc [19]) saw limited adoption. Competency questions are the standard form of this step [4]. | measured, association, literature |
| G2 | Every entity that a question starts from gets its own node or an index. Folding it into a property is not free. | Flat folds place, organisation and tag into properties. On the three questions that start from one of those (Q17 employees of an organisation, Q18 messages with a tag, Q19 people in a city) flat scans the graph: 3×, 768× and 26× the hops of mid, latency slope 0.8–1.1 against about 0 for mid. On the other seventeen, flat is at or below mid's cost. | measured |
| G3 | Reify a relation only where you need per-statement provenance or qualifiers, and budget for it: about 2× hops and latency, 2.5× memory, on every query that crosses it. | Normalized over mid: median hop ratio 2.03 (1.7–3.0 across the twenty queries), median latency 2.3×, memory 2.4–2.5× at every scale (H1 supported). The cost is a constant factor, not a higher order (H3 refuted). Wikidata attaches qualifiers and references to a statement instead of reifying a node [16]; Freebase's reified compound value types had no direct equivalent when the data had to move [11]. | measured, association |
| G4 | Default to entity nodes with direct edges (the mid schema). It is never the fastest and never the slowest, and it answers every question with bounded cost. | Mid is within 20% of the best schema on 12 of 20 queries, up to 4× slower where flat answers from a property, and one to three orders of magnitude faster on the reverse lookups (H4 partly refuted: 60%, not 80%). | measured |
| G5 | Measure at three or more scales and fit the slope. A prototype cannot show which design fails. | Q18 on flat takes 2 ms at 1,000 persons and looks acceptable; its latency slope is 1.09, so it grows with the whole graph. Neighbourhood queries (Q01–Q12, Q15, Q16, Q20) have slopes of 0.0–0.4 on every schema; only the path searches Q13 and Q14 grow with n everywhere (1.2–1.4), and no schema choice changes that. | measured |
| G6 | Record provenance on every statement from the first load. | `provenance-per-statement` appears in three sustained cases (Google and peers [15], Wikidata [16], Gene Ontology [18]). Freebase's per-statement sourcing was optional; at migration a large fraction of its facts could not be imported because the target required a source per statement [11]. | association |
| G7 | Check quality with soft constraints that run continuously and surface violations, not with a schema that blocks writes. | Wikidata declares constraints on properties and reports violations by bot [16]; Schema.org accepts imperfect data and lets consumers clean it [17]; Gene Ontology reasons over logical definitions to catch inconsistencies [18]; SNOMED CT classifies every release. All four are sustained. | association |
| G8 | Cap expressivity at what stays tractable, and give consumers subsets rather than the whole. | SNOMED CT limits itself to the EL description-logic profile so classification stays tractable, and most implementers use extracts (`expressivity-capped-for-tractability`, `subset-for-use`). Cyc's higher-order logic made inference expensive and results hard to predict, and adoption stayed limited [19]. | association |
| G9 | Obsolete terms instead of deleting them, and keep conflicting values with ranks instead of choosing one. | Gene Ontology obsoletes with replacement pointers and versions definitions as meaning drifts [18]; Wikidata keeps contradictory values with preferred / normal / deprecated ranks [16]. Both have run for more than two decades or one decade respectively under those rules. | association |
| G10 | Decide who edits the schema and how, and do not let the graph depend on one sponsor. | Editorial governance appears in Gene Ontology, SNOMED CT and the corporate graphs in [15]. Freebase was sustained by a single sponsor and ended when the sponsor's priorities changed (`platform-dependency`) [11]. Governance alone is not sufficient: Cyc had it and still saw limited adoption. | association |
| G11 | Put hop count, latency and memory on the design-review checklist. No existing quality framework measures them. | Of the seven frameworks in the RQ1 matrix, none has a metric for operational cost. OQuaRE [6] names performance efficiency but scores it from structural proxies. Application-based evaluation [7] is the only approach that would capture it and is the least standardized. | literature |
| G12 | When the domain is too heterogeneous for a fixed schema, discover the schema from the data and measure extraction quality as graph quality. | Amazon's product graph treats schema discovery as a learning problem because product types and attributes change continuously (`schema-discovered-not-designed`). Grade B source: a keynote, no peer-reviewed system paper. | association (grade B) |

## What this list does not say

- The experiment ran on one engine (an in-memory property graph) and synthetic data. Hop counts transfer to other engines; constant factors do not. A property index would remove flat's reverse-lookup penalty at the cost of maintaining one (see the threats section of the results).
- Eight purposive cases give associations. A pattern that co-occurs with survival is a reason to ask about it in a design review, not a guarantee.
- Guidelines from grade B or C sources are evidence of practice, not of effect.
